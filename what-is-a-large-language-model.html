What is a Large Language Model (LLM)?

👤 AI Academy Editorial Team 📅 Updated July 2026 ⏱ 14 min read

A Large Language Model (LLM) is a specialized deep learning model trained on massive text datasets to understand, summarize, generate, and reason with human natural language. LLMs power modern AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

To understand the neural architecture beneath LLMs, review How Neural Networks Work Step by Step.

The Transformer Revolution & Self-Attention

Before 2017, language models processed text sequentially word-by-word using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). The breakthrough Transformer architecture introduced the Self-Attention mechanism, allowing models to process entire sentences simultaneously and weigh relationships between distant words.

💡 How Self-Attention Works

In the sentence "The bank robber jumped into the river bank," self-attention mathematical vectors calculate that the first "bank" relates to money and the second "bank" relates to water geography based on surrounding context words.

Tokens & Context Windows Explained

LLMs do not read words as letters; they split raw text into mathematical sub-word units called tokens. On average, 1,000 tokens equal roughly 750 English words.

The Context Window defines the maximum number of tokens an LLM can hold in working memory during a conversation. Modern 2026 models support context windows ranging from 128,000 to over 2,000,000 tokens, allowing entire codebases or multi-hundred-page books to be ingested in a single prompt. Learn prompt optimization in Prompt Engineering Complete Guide.

The Three Stages of LLM Training

  1. Pre-training (Self-Supervised Learning): The model reads trillions of internet tokens and learns to predict the next word in a sequence. This phase builds general world knowledge.
  2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT): Human curators provide high-quality question-and-answer pairs so the raw text predictor learns to respond in a helpful Q&A format.
  3. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF): Human evaluators rate model answers to align output with safety, truthfulness, and helpfulness guidelines. Read more in AI Ethics and Bias.

Extending LLMs with AI Agents & RAG

Standard LLMs are limited to static training data. Modern applications pair LLMs with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to query live databases and integrate them into Autonomous AI Agents capable of taking real-world web actions.
