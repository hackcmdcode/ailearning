Natural Language Processing (NLP) Explained

👤 AI Academy Editorial Team 📅 Updated July 2026 ⏱ 12 min read

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is the branch of artificial intelligence that empowers computers to understand, interpret, analyze, and generate human language in both text and speech forms.

NLP forms the core engine powering search engines, automated translation tools, voice assistants, and modern Large Language Models (LLMs).

Traditional Text Preprocessing Pipeline

Before deep neural networks process text, raw documents pass through standardized cleaning stages:

  1. Tokenization: Breaking continuous text blocks into discrete word or sub-word tokens.
  2. Stop Word Removal: Stripping out high-frequency words (e.g., "and", "the", "is") that carry minimal unique semantic value.
  3. Stemming & Lemmatization: Reducing words to their root forms (e.g., "running", "ran", "runs" → "run").
  4. Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging: Labeling tokens as nouns, verbs, adjectives, or adverbs.

Vector Embeddings & Semantic Space

Computers cannot calculate text directly; words are mapped into high-dimensional vector spaces where mathematically similar concepts reside close together:

Core NLP Applications in Modern Software

  • Sentiment Analysis: Automatically detecting whether a customer review is positive, negative, or neutral.
  • Named Entity Recognition (NER): Identifying names of people, organizations, dates, and locations in unstructured reports.
  • Machine Translation: Converting natural language accurately between over 100 languages in real time.
  • Conversational Chatbots: Powering agents like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.
