Computer Vision Basics: How Machines See the World

👤 AI Academy Editorial Team 📅 Updated July 2026 ⏱ 10 min read

Computer Vision (CV) is a field of artificial intelligence that enables software to process, analyze, and extract meaningful information from digital images, video feeds, and 3D visual data. To a computer, an image is simply a 2D or 3D grid matrix of numeric pixel values (RGB channels from 0 to 255).

For a refresher on deep learning architectures, check out Machine Learning vs Deep Learning Explained.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)

Traditional neural networks struggle with high-resolution images because every pixel requires a separate input node. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) solve this by sliding small mathematical filter matrices (kernels) across the image to extract visual features:

  • Early Layers: Detect basic edges, color gradients, and corners.
  • Middle Layers: Combine edges to detect shapes, textures, and object parts (eyes, wheels).
  • Deep Layers: Synthesize complete objects (cars, faces, medical abnormalities).

The 4 Core Computer Vision Tasks

Task Objective Real-World Example
Image Classification Assign a single label to an entire image. Determining whether a photo contains a dog or cat.
Object Detection Locate objects and draw bounding boxes around them. Autonomous driving software tracking pedestrians and road signs.
Semantic Segmentation Classify every individual pixel in the image. Medical AI isolating tumor boundaries in MRI scans.
Facial & Pose Recognition Identify biometric keypoints and identity matrices. Smartphone facial unlock and security camera tracking.

Vision Transformers (ViT) & Multimodal AI

In modern 2026 AI systems, Vision Transformers (ViT) compete directly with traditional CNNs by treating image patches as visual tokens, enabling seamless multimodal models like Google Gemini to analyze video frames and text simultaneously. See physical applications in AI Robotics & Automation.
